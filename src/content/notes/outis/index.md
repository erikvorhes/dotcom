Back when Emily Wilson's translation of <cite>The Odyssey</cite> was announced, I eagerly preorded it, and when it arrived, I immediately fell in love with her translation of the opening lines:

<pre class="verse">Tell me about a complicated man.
Muse, tell me how he wandered and was lost
when he had wrecked the holy town of Troy,
and where he went, and who he met, the pain
he suffered in the storms at sea, and how
he worked to save his life and bring his men
back home. He failed to keep them safe; poor fools,
they ate the Sun God’s cattle, and the god
kep them from home. Now, goddess, child of Zeus,
tell the old story for our modern times.
Find the beginning.</pre>

TRANSITION

Stuff about getting distracted, setting it down until the nolan film came out.

Something about being mad at past me (or maybe previous translators?) for losing interest after Odysseus visits the underworld.

Maybe something about Wilson's reactions to the film, something about every re-creation is an interpretation, and wonder about what is changed or emphasized or erased.

I like it but am worried that it loses the wrong things by being so singularly focused at the end on a grand "civilization is ending" theme instead of focusing on the humanness of wanting to return to something that cannot be the way it was because time is relentless and nostalgia is dangerous (see Agamemnon).

I'm troubled by the absense of women aside from mostly bit parts, and the removal of their agency --- and how the world affects them (see the underworld, see Nausicaa, see scarred Helen, see Calypso as the therapist fuckbuddy on a literal desert shore & not among the abundance on her island). Perhaps the only woman done better by Nolan is Circe (the "back into your disguises" line still haunts me).

The Viking longships are a cool oddity. The filmic "intertextuality" is cool. The Cyclops is terrifying but an obvious missed opportunity.

Other pieces that I've enjoyed.
