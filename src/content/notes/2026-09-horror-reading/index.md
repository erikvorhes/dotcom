If I've interacted with you at all in (ahem) real life, I've probably tried to slip [<cite>The Buffalo Hunter Hunter</cite>](https://bookshop.org/p/books/the-buffalo-hunter-hunter-stephen-graham-jones/d1b4a8cac4757e4e) into conversation. Way back when it was announced, I put it on my "read when I can" list, and finally I got around to reading it. I normally don't do horror, but various folks whose opinions I respect seemed very excited by this book. Having read it now, I understand why. I won't go into detail here, because it's probably worth knowing very little about it before you start reading it. The novel involves the discovery of an old journal and large excerpts from that journal, including a story related to the journal-keeper. It's a doubly-nested story, and Stephen Graham Jones sticks the landing. It's profound and tense and funny --- and it's extremely hard not to tell people about it.

My enjoyment of <cite>The Buffalo Hunter Hunter</cite> inspired me to give horror more of a chance than I typically have; I had convinced myself that the genre wasn't really my thing.
